How this works

Updated automatically, every week

A scheduled job runs every Monday. It refreshes the official death counts from the CDC and San Francisco open-data APIs, reads the San Francisco Medical Examiner’s newest monthly letter for the deaths involving each newer drug, checks the agencies that publish such counts, then searches for new reports of deaths, confirms each count against its source and adds those that qualify. Reports published in the past week carry a yellow ring on the map.

Trusted primary sources only

Every report links to the organization that counted the deaths: medical examiners and coroners, health departments, the CDC and the White House drug policy office. News coverage alone does not qualify.

Points, not a heat map

A few dozen reports would make a heat map of where news happens, not where people overdose. Each point is one report of deaths, placed approximately and coloured by the drug it names first. For how many people are dying, use the official counts above.

Data and corrections

The data files are public: incidents, trend and compounds. Structures are PubChem’s 2D records, drawn at one bond length. Official counts come from CDC state counts, CDC county counts and the San Francisco Medical Examiner. Spotted a mistake? Open an issue.

Harm-reduction information, not medical advice. If someone won’t wake up or isn’t breathing normally, call 911 and give Narcan.
