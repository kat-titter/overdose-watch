How this works

What counts as a report

Overdose deaths and clusters, public-health alerts, drug-checking findings, seizures that name a substance in California or Nevada, official data releases, and policy changes that affect Narcan or new drugs. Reports from other states are included only when they concern a new substance or a national alert.

Why points, not a heat map

A few dozen reports of very different kinds (one death, a statewide data release, a seizure) would make a heat map that shows where news coverage happens, not where people overdose. Each point is one report with its source. For how many people are dying, and whether that is falling, use the official counts at the top.

How it updates

Every Monday an AI-assisted search (Claude) looks through news, health departments, medical examiners, the DEA and CDC, and drug-checking programs, checks each new report against its source, and adds it here. The official counts are refreshed from the CDC and San Francisco open-data APIs at the same time. Spotted a mistake? Open an issue.

Sources

Death counts: CDC NCHS provisional counts (states and US), CDC county counts, and the San Francisco Medical Examiner’s monthly counts. Reports: each card links its own source. Data files: incidents.json and trend.json.

Harm-reduction information, not medical advice. If someone won’t wake up or isn’t breathing normally, call 911 and give Narcan. Positions on the map are approximate; posts from X appear only when they surface in web search.
